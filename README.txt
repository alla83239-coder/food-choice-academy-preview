Food Choice Academy — Healthy & Sustainable School Canteen

Project purpose
A CLIL / English-learning simulation in which students plan a healthy, affordable and environmentally responsible school canteen for teenagers aged 15–18.

Project journey
- Investigate adolescent nutrition and macronutrient reference ranges.
- Compare food choices and greenhouse-gas emissions.
- Design a one-day menu for breakfast, lunch, snack and drinks.
- Calculate portion-level energy, protein, carbohydrates, fat and fibre using verified food-composition data; compare macronutrient energy shares with age-group reference ranges.
- Build a carbon-aware procurement basket with quantities, simulated practice prices and estimated kg CO2e.
- Select a vending-machine assortment, with storage and allergen information.
- Plan the canteen layout, equipment, service flow and opening timeline.
- Prepare a detailed 20-slide project presentation.

Project files
- index.html: project entry point
- styles.css: shared visual system, responsive layouts and printable project slides
- app.js: missions, language activities, menu planner, footprint calculators, procurement, vending assortment, launch plan and final presentation
- assets/audio/: supplied course audio
- assets/video/: supplied course video

Open index.html in a modern browser. Audio and video assets are stored locally in assets/. Progress is saved in the browser used to open the project.

Evidence and source policy
- Health Canada Dietary Reference Intakes are used for population-level macronutrient ranges for ages 4–18. These ranges include 15–18-year-olds but are not individual prescriptions.
- WHO guidance is used for public-health context on fruit and vegetables, fibre and free sugars.
- Food-emissions factors are global averages based on Poore & Nemecek (2018), presented by Our World in Data. They are not supplier-specific measurements.
- Practice prices are illustrative and must be replaced with supplier quotations during real procurement.
- Academy-created reading material is labelled as such. It is not presented as a verbatim Workbook extract.
- The final project presentation distinguishes saved student decisions from information that still requires verification.
